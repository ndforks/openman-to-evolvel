## What you may do without asking

- Use the names in plain text to refer truthfully to the project or to Orgo, for example "a fork of OpenMan",
  "based on OpenMan", "works with Orgo" or "runs on Orgo cloud computers".
- Keep the copyright notice, the LICENSE, the NOTICE file and this policy in your copy, as the licence requires.
- Run an unmodified or modified copy privately, for yourself or inside your own organisation, without renaming it.
- Write articles, tutorials, reviews and talks about OpenMan or Orgo that use the names accurately.

## What you may not do

- Distribute a modified version, or offer any version as a hosted service to people outside your own organisation,
  under the name "OpenMan" or "Orgo" or with their logos.
- Use a name, logo, domain name or social media handle that is confusingly similar to the Marks (for example

- Suggest that your product, fork or service is official, certified, endorsed or supported by Organic Intelligence,
  Inc. when it is not.
- Use the Marks in a way that is misleading, disparaging, or that breaks the law.

## Forks that are distributed or hosted must rebrand

If you publish a modified version, ship it to others, or run it as a service for people outside your own
organisation, you must:

1. Choose a different product name and replace the logos. The brand lives in one place:
   `src/config/brand.ts` (name, wordmark, mode labels) and `public/brand/` (logo files). Also review user-facing text
   in `content/` and in the marketing pages.
2. Make clear that your product is not OpenMan and is not provided by Organic Intelligence, Inc. You may say that it
   is "based on OpenMan".
3. Keep the copyright notice and the LICENSE as the MIT License requires.

Using Orgo's service as your compute provider is fine and does not need permission beyond Orgo's own terms of
service. Saying "runs on Orgo" in plain text is fine; using the Orgo logo as if Orgo provided your product is not.
